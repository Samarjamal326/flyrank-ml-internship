Ranking Signal Analysis

Understanding which content signals drive search visibility on FlyRank

Abstract

This paper investigates the relationship between safe content signals and search visibility using the FlyRank dataset. We compare a gradient‑boosting model against a simple rule‑based baseline, evaluate precision@20 and @50, and surface the most influential signals. The results inform actionable recommendations for improving content ranking.

Introduction

Search ranking decisions are routinely based on a mixture of relevance, authority, and freshness signals. For product teams deciding where to invest editorial effort, understanding which signals truly move content into the top‑visible tier is critical. This capstone provides an evidence‑backed answer for FlyRank.

Data

We use the public FlyRank ML Internship dataset (released on Hugging Face). The snapshot includes anonymized content IDs, 90‑day impressions, clicks, CTR, freshness and authority scores, keyword groups, and publish dates. Rows with missing mandatory fields were dropped. The data spans 2023‑01‑01 to 2023‑06‑30.

Methodology

We engineer log‑scaled impressions, clicks, and CTR, and one‑hot encode keyword groups. The target is a binary “high visibility” label defined as the top 10 % of pages by impressions. We split the data 70 %/30 % (random, stratified, seed 42) to mirror the Week‑4 baseline. A HistGradientBoostingClassifier (max_depth 6, learning_rate 0.1) is trained. The baseline is a simple rule: pages with impressions above the median.

Limitations & Honest Framing

All metrics are computed on a single hold‑out split; variance across random seeds is modest but not quantified. The model does not incorporate user‑level signals, so findings apply to aggregate content performance only. Results should be used directionally to prioritize signal engineering rather than as a deterministic ranking rule.
